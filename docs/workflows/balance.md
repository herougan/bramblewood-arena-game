# Workflow: balance analysis and simulations

For the budget formula, card retunes, the road and difficulty curves, raid tuning and win-rate measurement. The owner wants the strongest model for balance work.

## Files you need
- `bramblewood-budget.js`: N(L) and the stat checks. It is the source of truth for the formula.
- `bramblewood-engine.js`: simulations run the real engine headless through `tests/lib/harness.js`.
- Tools:
  - `tools/card_balance.js` and `tools/card_balance_report.py`: win rate per card.
  - `tools/difficulty_curve.js`, `tools/curve_tune.js` and `tools/autotune_map.js`: the road and difficulty curves.
  - `tools/forge_curve_2026_10_19.js`: Forge costs and map placement.
  - `tools/cpu_levels.js`.
- Data and outputs: `docs/balance/*.json` (difficulty curve, post-balance queue, checkpoints). The owner's live tweaks are in the hub's Budget & stats tab (db `budgetEdits`, `cardEdits`, `monsterEdits`); fold them with `tools/hub/apply_balance_edits.js`.

## How to work
- Measure, then change. Simulations are expensive. Run them on the smallest card set that answers the question, save the raw results to `docs/balance/`, and reuse them while the engine and cards haven't changed.
- Run simulations alone on the machine (2 cores). Never run them alongside other agents' builds or test suites.
- Every number in a report comes from a run in this session or a saved result file named in the report.
- Changes to card stats follow `card-data.md`. Changes to the formula update `bramblewood-budget.js` and its test (`tests/level-curve-2026-10-19.js`, `tests/forge-curve-2026-10-19.js`).

## Measuring a skill's worth: the standard test (owner, 2026-10-21 16:33)
Use this setup for any new skill-worth measurement. It is fast and every result is comparable.
- **Test side:** a deck of 3/10 units. Each unit has either:
  - one random skill;
  - the skill being measured at different values;
  - no skill (the vanilla control).
- **Standard enemy:** a deck of 4/11 units with no abilities. Each turn, the enemy bramble gives one random creature of theirs +1/+0 or +0/+1.
- **Brambles:** both have 35 Health and no abilities.
- **Mono-test:** every test card has exactly 1 skill. **Duo-test:** every test card has exactly 2 skills, which measures how pairs of skills work together.
- **Runs:** 10,000 fights per setup. A skill's worth is its win-rate gain over the vanilla 3/10 deck against the same standard enemy, converted to budget points the way tools/skill_worth.js converts its deltas.
- **Robust mode** (package ST, owner 2026-10-22 18:20: "lumber, wait, multi-skill ... Field effects of various types"): `tools/skill_test_robust.js --mono|--duo|--multi`. Each unit of both decks draws a rarity / Wait 0-3 / Lumber 0-3 profile from the live band cards and gets its stats from the budget (Level 1, the skill's S priced in; the enemy 0.8x a vanilla unit's points, `--foe-ratio`, since at the owner's 19/16 the vanilla deck never wins). Each fight rolls its field from `FIELD_CONFIG` at the top of the tool: Day/Night and Tide 20% each, each uncommon field 2%, each rare one 0.2%, and 20% of fights get one more non-common field (classes from `--census`, `docs/balance/field-census-2026-10-22.json`). `--multi`: each unit carries the skill and 0-2 live companions, read as the marginal worth (with minus without). The report gives per skill its net (+ = under-priced), the stat points paid, the worth, and the worth per field condition. `--seed`, `--games` (10,000), `--jobs 2`, `--skills`, `--quick` (smoke). Output: `docs/balance/skill-test-robust-<modes>-<date>.json` + `.md`. The full sweep takes most of a day on 2 cores; run it alone.
- Fixed-number skills (damage, heals, armor, +N stats) are priced by the amount they deal. Relative skills (that multiply or copy the unit's own stats, speed or odds) scale with stats. When stats change scale, re-measure the relative ones first.

## Pricing by logic: the owner's 4-round rule (owner, 2026-10-23 20:44)
"balancing - just logic is sufficient. To me, in my head, i compare to the effect of 1 attack or health across 4 rounds to estimate. On play arrow is equivalent to only N damage across 4 rounds but it has the advantage of on play!"
- Yardstick: +1 Attack or +1 Health held over a typical 4-round life. Price a skill by what it does over those 4 rounds in that currency, then adjust for timing (on play, first strike, Wait) and reliability (conditions, odds).
- Example: On Play Arrow N deals N once, so it is worth less than +N Attack over 4 rounds, plus a premium for landing at once.
- Measurement (the robust skill test) is optional: use it when cheap; otherwise price by this logic and say so in the cost entry's note.

## Deck optimizer (package OPT, owner 2026-10-23 20:44)
The strongest deck per map, for building skirmishes of rising difficulty, with no Claude tokens: `tools/opt/deckopt.js`.
- Run: `node tools/opt/deckopt.js --map 3 --budget 16000 --workers 2` (or `--maps 0-3`, or `--minutes 20`; `--help` lists every option). Output: `docs/balance/opt/deckopt-map<NN>-<id>-<tag>.json` and `deckopt-summary-<tag>.md`.
- Your level is 1 (owner OPT-3: "the optimum (at lvl 1) deck first"); `--level ref` uses the game's reference level (0 on Maps 0-5).
- Phase 1 searches the map's cards plus what a player owns by then. Which cards those are depends on the mode: see "Pool modes" below. The OPT2 runs were `classic`: `canonical/map-pools.json` MAP + every submap tier, plus the HUB3 plan's advancement.
- Phase 2 (package OPT2, owner OPT-1/OPT-2 "use logic first ... that really limits the number of cards to prob 5-25"): a logic-filtered candidate list, 5-25 cards a map, each printed with why it qualified (`--candidates-only` prints the lists with no games). The rules are a small config, `tools/opt/phase2-rules.json`: points for the map's biome or a kin biome, a fight's terrain types, a unit type or animal group the map's cards share, a skill family the map's cards share (ranged on an arrow map) and a family the field rewards (Flyers in wind, Tide cards on a Tide map); never a later map's leaders, showcased cards, boss-only cards or boss / ender rewards, strange cards (hidden, Easter eggs, raid, world boss, Devilry, spells, equipment, awaiting a source ...), challenge cards, or cards first obtainable after Map 39 (a faction card waits for its rarity's gate). Every candidate is screened as a swap into the phase-1 deck, then the search goes on over map cards + candidates. `--cross-share` (default 0.3) is phase 2's share of the budget; `--cross-pool plan` restores the old every-obtainable-card phase 2.
- The search (`--method`, default `ga`): the genetic search with `--restarts 2` (each its own population) and niching (elites kept at least `--niche-radius 5` copies apart, `--niche-cap 2` a niche), or `descent`: one copy swapped at a time, 10 sampled neighbours under successive halving, first improvement, a local maximum after 3 samples without a gain, then a restart. `--compare` runs both on the map cards at equal `--budget` and scores their best-so-far decks on independent seeds (`deckopt-compare-<tag>.md`).
- The outputs per map: the optimum, the **distinct optima** (at least 6 copies apart, within 10 points of the best) with their mutual head-to-head, and the **difficulty ladder**: legal decks from the same pool sharing at least `--ladder-core 10` copies with the optimum, at Elo gaps 600 / 450 / 300 / 150 below it (`--ladder`), measured head to head against the optimum under the map's fields. A target the walks cannot reach is shown as "(nearest)".
- The gauntlet: each fight deck and the reference decks in a fair mirror under that fight's field (your level and bramble on both sides, CPU skill 1, seats swapped), plus the fights as the game builds them (`--road-weight` 0.25). "Points below the optimum": the optimum's head-to-head score against each fight's deck as Elo, and each fight deck's own score as your deck.
- Deterministic: `--budget` (search games) and `--final-games` (the output's `reproduce` line) give the same result on any machine and any worker count; `--minutes` turns into a game budget from the measured games/s. Checkpoints per stage (a restart, the screen, phase 2, the final, the optima, the ladder); `--resume` skips the finished ones.
- Without Claude: `bash tools/opt/build-exe.sh` builds one executable (Node SEA) that runs from a game checkout; `.github/workflows/deckopt.yml` runs it on GitHub's free runners by hand (Actions → Deck optimizer → Run workflow) and uploads the results as an artifact.
- Like every sim, run it alone on the machine; its games/s fall when other agents' builds or browser tests share the 2 cores.
- **Pool modes** (`--mode`, package PRO2, owner 2026-10-24 21:20 #9-#11). The cap is the rarest card any of the map's fights may hold (`roadRarityCap` over its fights): Map 0 Common, Maps 1-3 Uncommon (`--rarity-cap skirmish` uses a plain skirmish's cap instead).
  - `map-true` (the default): the map's own cards (MAP tier, SPECIAL tier and fight decks) plus what a player holds by then. That means the plan's owned cards, earlier main maps' MAP tiers and the cards of submaps already open. Every card must be within the cap, and an outside card must be first obtainable by this map. Phase 2 keeps the same rule, so it is usually empty and phase 1 gets the whole budget.
  - `bpro` (`--bpro`): the map's own pool only (MAP + SPECIAL + fight decks), within the cap. No phase 2.
  - `classic` (`--classic`): the OPT2 pool. It takes every submap's list as the map's cards, even a side map that opens 20 maps later, and owned cards at any rarity. That is how the Ent (the Old Bramble's Rare) reached 0-PRO-3.
  - Each output records `settings.mode`, the cap and what the rule left out (`pools.excluded`). `mapRuleProblems(gm, map, deck, mode)` checks a deck.
- **Faction cards are out of PRO pools** (package PRO3, owner 10-24 22:28 #3: "Don't include the faction cards into this"). `map-true` and `bpro` leave them out by default (`--no-faction`, the default; `--faction` keeps them; `settings.faction` in the output). A faction card is a faction-pack card: `source.kind: "pack"` with `tier: 11` in `canonical/cards.json` (the game's `factionPoolCardIds`; the HUB3 plan's `factionOtter` / `factionHummingbird` buckets). The `faction: "base"` / `"hummingbirds"` field on starter and tutorial cards (Otter Kit, Mosswing ...) does not make a faction card. The map rule carries it, so `pro_skirmishes.js` drops a PRO deck that holds one.
- **Side maps** (PRO3, owner #11: "PRO not done for Map 1-20 yet and its submaps"): `--side` adds each listed main map's side maps (not the tutorial, the endless dungeons or reserved maps), e.g. `--maps 0-20 --side`; a side map id works with `--map` too. A side map's own cards are its parent's MAP tier + its SUBMAP list + SPECIAL + its fight decks; what a player holds is counted at the map it opens on (`mapUnlockNumberOf`); its cap comes from its own fights. Outputs keep the parent's number: `deckopt-map02-mg-<tag>.json`.

## PRO fights (package PRO, owner 2026-10-24 00:28)
Each main map has 1-4 hidden skirmishes, `<map>-PRO-<k>` (e.g. `3-PRO-1`), with the best decks the optimizer found: `canonical/pro-skirmishes.json`, baked into the game by `assemble_arena.py`. They are not on the map, not counted for progress and pay nothing; Admin (🏆 PRO fights), the skirmish editor and `#pro=3-PRO-1` open them.
- Two kinds (package PRO2, owner 21:20 #11). `N-PRO-k` comes from the optimizer's `map-true` runs: the map's cards plus what a player holds by then, within the map's rarity cap. `N-BPRO-k` (Basic Pro, under the map's `bpro`) comes from its `bpro` runs: only the map's own pool, within the cap. Runs from before PRO2 (mode `classic`) are never candidates. A deck already in the file that breaks the map rule is dropped to `replaced`, with its reasons, and the map is filled again. The game bakes only `pro` until the PRO preview package wires `bpro` in (see that package's notes in `docs/CHANGELOG.md`).
- Routine, after an optimizer run: `node tools/opt/deckopt.js --maps 0-3 --budget 16000 --workers 2 --tag <tag>-maptrue` and `node tools/opt/deckopt.js --maps 0-3 --bpro --budget 16000 --workers 2 --tag <tag>-bpro` → `node tools/opt/pro_skirmishes.js` (dry run: read the plan; `--kind pro|bpro` for one kind) → `node tools/opt/pro_skirmishes.js --apply` → rebuild (`python3 assemble_arena.py && mv bramblewood-arena.html index.html`) → `node tests/run.js --only 'pro'` → release with the batch.
- The rule (`--help`): candidates are every run's distinct optima for the map (renamed card ids mapped, illegal decks skipped). A map's first fill takes the newest run's optimum as PRO-1. After that PRO-1 is replaced only by a candidate that beats it head to head at 55% or more over at least 400 games with its 95% interval above 50% (`--replace-at`, `--games`). Other decks within 100 Elo of PRO-1 (`--band`) and at least 6 copies apart (`--distinct`) become PRO-2.., best first, at most 4 a map (`--max`); they are renumbered by Elo. Replaced decks are kept under the map's `replaced`.
- Head to head: a fair mirror under the PRO's own field, both decks at Level 1, CPU skill 1 (Smart), seats swapped, no leaders, seeded by the two decks (a pairing always gives the same result).
- The field ("the average skirmish of that map"): per field rule (day / night, Mist, Tide, wind, terrain tier, ...) the value most of the map's fights have; a tie goes to the boss's (the map's last fight when it has no boss). From Map 2 a PRO fight leads with its deck's showcase card, as the map's skirmishes do.
- Each PRO lists `later`: its cards first obtainable after the map (phase 2's related cards can be). Report it; the deck stays.
- Side maps (PRO3): a side map with its own runs gets its own entry under its id, named after its fight codes, `N-L-PRO-k` / `N-L-BPRO-k` (Smugglers' Grotto's fights are 2-B-1.., so `2-B-PRO-1`). The game shows a side map's own entry on that side map (`proEntryIdOf`; one without shows its parent's). `--maps` takes main map numbers and covers their side maps.
- PRO3's runs: `node tools/opt/deckopt.js --maps 0-20 --side --budget 16000 --ladder none --workers 2 --tag 2026-10-25-pro3-maptrue` and the same with `--bpro` (tag `...-pro3-bpro`). `--ladder none` because the PRO tool reads only the distinct optima; the ladder is about a third of a map's games.

## Card contribution (package BAL6, K5, owner 10-27 11:22)
"show the general 'contribution' of each card with our myriad testing ... maybe test w 30 hp brambles w random effects": `tools/balance/contribution.js`.
- One fight: two random legal 20-card decks (6-14 different cards, copies within the rarity's copy cap and the top-rank caps) from every playable card (any source; not tokens, tests, heroes, Hall of Fame, leaders, spells, Materia, equipment or Easter eggs); both brambles a plain 30 Health (in-engine, no abilities); a field drawn from the maps' rule pool (every road fight's `fightRules`, one entry a fight); Smart CPU on both sides; Level 1; no leaders; seats swapped every other fight. Fight i is a pure function of `--seed` and i, so any worker count gives the same result.
- Measures per card: raw Δ (win rate of decks holding it minus decks without it); per copy (a ridge regression of the result on the two decks' copy-count difference: one copy against an average copy, the rest of the deck controlled for; sandwich standard errors); contribution (per copy x the copies a deck usually holds); vs its rarity band's median (starter, common, uncommon, rare, very rare and up) with z. The top 20 each way are flagged. Confidence: split-half reliability.
- Run: `node tools/balance/contribution.js --games 135000 --workers 2 --resume` (about 90 minutes; checkpoints in `<tmp>/contribution`); `--check` (no games: deck legality, determinism, the solver); `--quick` (2,000 fights); `--analyze` re-runs the analysis from the saved outcomes. Output: `docs/balance/contribution-<date>.json` + `.md`; the hub's 🗃️ Card table → 🏅 Contribution view reads the newest one.
- Read it as strength on its own: a card that needs partners (a tribe, Lumber makers) reads low in random decks.

## The balance workbench (package TOOLS1, owner 10-28 21:40)
"we need the following applications: 1) a good way to measure cards contribution 2) a good way to quickly measure skills impact to a card's contribution and therefore indirectly its strength - in a fair and balanced way 3) a good way to optimise a deck out of a list of cards, say, 15 cards." One command line, `node tools/bench/bench.js <cmd>` (`help <cmd>` lists the options). Measurement only: it never writes cards, decks or `canonical/skill-costs.json`.
- **The paired swap** (contrib and skill). Fight i of a context is a pure function of its seed and i: a random legal opponent deck, a random legal rest of 20 - k cards and a field. A slot of k copies (`--copies`, default 6; an instrument, not a legal deck) completes the rest: the card, a variant of it (a skill removed or added, +1 Attack, +1 Health) or k Blanks (0/1, 0 Lumber, no skill). Every variant plays the same fights with the same engine seed and deck order, so differences are paired. When the rest holds the measured card, its copies go to other cards first. Level, brambles (30 Health), Smart CPU on both sides and the seat swap are BAL6's.
- **Contexts.** Default: every fieldable card of BAL6 as the pool and every road fight's field, Level 1. `--map N`: the map's own cards (deckopt's `bpro` pool, `--mode map-true` for the wider one), its fights' fields, its reference level. `--pool a,b,c` replaces the pool.
- **The cache.** Every game is kept in `<tmp>/bramble-bench/bench-<context>-<key>.json` (`--cache-dir`), keyed by the game's fingerprint (engine, app, cards, budget, map pools) and the tool's version, so a re-run plays only what is missing: a stopped long run resumes by re-running the same command, and the Blank games of the base sample are shared by every card. A card or engine change starts a new cache.
- **T1 `contrib`**: `node tools/bench/bench.js contrib --cards owl-nightwatch` (about 15 s a card at 200 fights; `--map 3`; `--all`, `--save`). Per card: the contribution of k copies over k Blanks ± 95%, per copy, value per budget point (per copy / N at the level played) and its index (/ the median card), and the distance from the fair line (per copy = a + b x N fitted over the cards measured; + stronger than its budget buys). Output with `--all` / `--save`: `docs/balance/bench-contrib-<context>-<date>.json` + `.md`. BAL6's regression (`tools/balance/contribution.js`) stays the all-cards-at-once view in the 🗃️ Card table.
- **T2 `skill`**: `node tools/bench/bench.js skill arrow` (a trigger entry: `T:onReady>gain`, quoted in a shell), or `--all`. Per skill up to `--carriers` cards that carry it (default 3; the --all run of 10-28 used 2); a skill on fewer than `--min-real` (2) cards also gets two vanilla carriers (a Common and an Uncommon / Rare with no skill, nearest their full budget) given the skill as its card has it (`--no-synthetic` skips those skills instead). Per carrier: with the skill and without it at the same stats; as printed with +1 Attack and with +1 Health. The bumps pooled over every carrier give the yardstick, win-rate points per budget point (Attack 2 points, Health 1: the budget's 2A + H; an Archer's Attack bump is left out). **Worth** = the skill's paired gain / the yardstick, in budget points (Attack-equivalents = worth / 2); **price** = the carrier's (2A + H) x m + S with the skill minus without it (`bramblewood-budget.js`); **net** = worth - price (+ under-priced); confidence high |z| >= 2.5, medium >= 1.5. Per N where the skill has a number. `--all` writes `docs/balance/bench-skills-<date>.json` + `.md` and `bench-skill-prices-<date>.json` (suggested table values for the medium / high rows; logic-priced and trigger entries get points only). A run of one skill borrows the last `--all` run's yardstick when the game is the same. `--plan` prints the carriers and the variant count without playing.
- **T3 `deck`**: `node tools/bench/bench.js deck --map 3` (`--pool a,b,c` for any list; `--max-unique 6 --max-copies 4 --size 20`, the owner's step-4 rule; `--budget` 9000 search games, about 8 minutes on 2 cores). The gauntlet is deckopt's (the map's fight decks and reference decks in a fair mirror under each fight's field, at the map's level; `--road-weight` adds the fights as the game builds them). Focuses, each only if the list supports it (`--focus` picks): open, big (8+ copies of the list's costliest step), economy (6+ copies of Lumber / Stone / Metal makers), stone / metal (6+ copies paid in them), swarm (14+ copies of the cheapest step) and the list's two biggest skill families (`tools/opt/phase2-rules.json`). Each focus: seed decks, then a descent of constrained neighbours under deckopt's successive halving; each focus's deck keeps at least `--apart` (6) copies from the earlier ones (niching). Output: each deck's win rate ± 95% and per opponent, average Lumber, curve, labels read from its curve and skills; `docs/balance/bench-deck-map<NN>-<id>-<date>.json` + `.md`.
- **Checks**: `node tools/bench/bench.js check` (about 40 s), run by `tests/tools1-2026-10-28.js`.
- **The hub**: the 🧪 Workbench section of the 🧬 Deck optimizer tab reads the newest `bench-skills-*.json`, `bench-contrib-global-*.json` and `bench-deck-*.md` (`tools/hub/bench_data.py`).
- **Reading it.** Random decks: a skill or card that needs partners (a tribe, Lumber makers, a field it rarely meets) reads low. A low-confidence skill is "not measurably mispriced at this many fights", not "fair". Re-measure the flagged skills at more fights (`skill <id> --games 400 --carriers 4`) before changing a price; prices change only on the owner's word.

## Gem sockets are outside the budget (package H1, owner decision H1, 2026-10-24)
Unique and rarer cards have one Materia gem socket per owned copy (the Forge's 💎 Socket tab). A socketed gem's stats (`ENCHANTS` in `arena_app.js`: Ember +1 Attack, Tide +2 Health, Grove +1/+1, Stone +3 Health) are a progression bonus, like Forge levels: they sit outside the card's printed budget. `bramblewood-budget.js` and the stat checks price the card as printed (no gem), and simulations, the deck optimizer and the PRO fights run without gems. When a gem's effect is retuned, price it with the 4-round rule above and keep it small next to a Forge level; the costs to set and take out a gem are in `canonical/upgrade-costs.json` `materiaSocket` (hub 💰 Upgrade costs tab).

## Pipeline (package SPEED1, owner 2026-10-31: "balancing runs as automation, not agent tokens")
One command a step, no Claude tokens while it runs. An agent starts a step (or `all`), waits, then reads only the printed summary (at most 20 lines) and the flagged items, not the tools' output. `tools/pipeline/run.sh <step> [options]`; the steps wrap the SKILL2 tools, they do not replace them. Config (stages, thresholds, owner cards, the known test failures): `tools/pipeline/config.json`.

| Step | Command | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| measure | `run.sh measure --skills fork,armor=4,armor=5 [--map N] [--minutes M] [--force]` | `tools/skill_canon_2026_10_31.js` on the plan of `tools/skill2_measure_2026_10_31.js`. `id` = every plan item of the skill; `id=N` = that item, or an ad hoc one when no in-play card carries it. `--map N` = the skills on map N's own context (stored apart, recorded only). No `--skills` = every plan item without a result for this game. Results: `docs/balance/pipeline/measures.json`. |
| price | `run.sh price [--dry] [--set-const cap2SScale=0.5,flying=1.13,evasive=1.13]` | `tools/skill2_apply_2026_10_31.js prices` on the SKILL2 measures with the pipeline's laid over them, into `canonical/skill-costs.json`. `--set-const` writes a budget constant, only those named, only by value (`cap2SScale`, `flying`, `evasive`, `shadow`): an owner call, with a dated comment in `bramblewood-budget.js`. A half-miss measure that disagrees with its constant is flagged, never written on its own. |
| refit | `run.sh refit [--dry]` | The smallest stat change per in-play card the new prices (or constants) pushed out of tolerance (BUDGET2's rule, `tools/skill2_refit_2026_10_31.js`). Rule-clean ones are written to `canonical/cards.json`; the rest are flagged and not applied (below). |
| bake | `run.sh bake [--dry] [--no-bpro] [--bpro-all] [--minutes M]` | `tools/opt/skill2_pro_rebake_2026_10_31.js` (fields and showcase leaders), then `tools/opt/bpro2_2026_10_31.js` for the maps whose inputs changed (engine, search tools, stats and skills of the cards on the map's list), `apply`, `check`. Rebuild the game afterwards. |
| tournament | `run.sh tournament --games 24 [--maps 0-20] [--tag T]` | `tools/opt/ref5_prog.js measure road`: the Map reference and the five progression decks against every road fight; mean win per deck, fights more than 0.25 from the road target flagged. Not part of `all` (add `--tournament`). |
| test | `run.sh test [--only regex] [--full]` | `node tests/run.js --smoke`, then the balance `--only` set; passed / failed / cached counted from the runner; failures in the known list are reported as known, any other as NEW. |
| all | `run.sh all --skills ... [--set-const ...]` | measure, price, refit, bake, test in that order; stops at the first failed step (and before `price` if `measure` is partial). |

- **The lock.** Heavy steps (measure, bake, tournament, test, all) take one `flock` (`bramble-pipeline.lock` in the repository's common `.git` folder, shared by every checkout; `PIPELINE_LOCK` overrides). A second heavy call exits 75 and says who holds it; `--wait` (or `--wait SECONDS`) waits instead. price and refit run any time. Never start a sim by hand beside a running step.
- **Early stopping.** `measure` plays each item in stages of 200, 400, 700 and 1,000 paired fights and stops when the 95% interval of its worth is as narrow as `high` confidence in the price tool (se <= max(0.3, 15% of the worth)). Fights are cached by the game's fingerprint, so a later stage plays only what it adds, and an item whose result is on file for the same game is not played again. The anchors are shared by every item of a context. Any change to `canonical/cards.json`, the budget file or the engine changes the fingerprint and starts a new cache: measure before you change them.
- **What each summary holds** (`docs/balance/pipeline/<step>-<YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM>.json`, schema `bramblewood-pipeline/1`): `status` (ok / partial / fail), `durationSec`, `inputs` (fingerprint, files, settings), `counts`, `changed` (id, field, old -> new), `flagged` (id, why), `cached` (reused / played), `tests` (passed, failed, cached, knownFailures, newFailures), `files`. `all` adds `stages` (each step's status, time and file). Working files (logs, the table before a price step, the merged measures, the plans) are in `docs/balance/pipeline/state/`.
- **Refit flags** (never applied; each carries its proposal): a named owner card (`config.json` refit.ownerCards: BUDGET2-5, DECK1-1, DECK1-5, RECOST1-5, the Dragons); no stat line fits or the plan holds the card; more than 2 stat points change; theme clash (the card changes body shape, hitter / balanced / wall, or loses its last point of Attack); Anadromous carriers (ENG15).

**When an agent should look.** Read the 20 lines; open the file only for these:
1. `status` is `fail` or `partial` (read `notes`; `partial` = run the same command again).
2. `flagged` is not empty. measure: interval still wide at 1,000 fights, or a skill more than 1 point off its price. price: a half-miss constant that disagrees with its measure. refit: each flagged card is an owner or design call (decide, then edit `canonical/cards.json` by hand per `card-data.md`). bake: a BPRO best score that fell, or a failed `check`. tournament: a fight far from the road target.
3. `tests.newFailures` is not empty. `knownFailures` is the coordinator's list in `config.json`; keep it current.
4. Owner calls (a constant, a held card, a named card) go to `docs/decisions.json`.
Otherwise take the summary as the report: the changed cards are `changed` in the refit file, the new prices `changed` in the price file.

## Checks
`node tests/run.js --only 'level-curve|forge-curve|card-pass|raid-rb|raid-br|card-pool'`, then the card matrix. Reviewed golden changes are accepted at release.

## Done when
There is a short report: what moved, by how much, and why, with the data file named. Owner calls go to `docs/decisions.json`. Only the result doc goes to the Project, not the working notes.
