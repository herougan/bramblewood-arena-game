## Method

- **Test creatures** are defined in the tool only, never in `canonical/cards.json`. They are Common, Wait 0, Map 0, Level 1. Each is sized by `bramblewood-budget.js`: N = `nOf` (the plain bonus included), and the body is the largest 3A + H inside cap 1 with A·H inside cap 2 (the more even shape on a tie). The vanillas are 0 Lumber 2/7 (N 13.57), 1 Lumber 3/7 (16.87), 2 Lumber 3/11 (20.67) and 3 Lumber 4/12 (24.97).
- **A-0** holds 12 of the 0-Lumber vanilla, 5 of the 1-Lumber, 2 of the 2-Lumber and 1 of the 3-Lumber (20 cards). **A-n** replaces n of the 12 zero-cost vanillas with a resource test creature. That creature has the 0-Lumber vanilla's body (2/7) plus one skill, and costs 0 Lumber. The every-turn skills also get a second variant that costs 1 Lumber. n = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12.
- **Skills**, all the resource and income skills and triggers on Maps 0-20 (`docs/effects-cards-0-20.md`, Resources and the gain triggers): Delivery 1, Forestry 1, Foraging 1, On Play: gain 1 Lumber, On Attack: gain 1, On Kill: gain 1, On Attacked: gain 1, Bounty 1. On Healed: gain (Gannet Nestweaver) gets no test creature, because nothing in the test decks heals. The card is measured in A-0 only.
- **Placebo:** a test creature with a key the engine does not know. It measures the CPU's bias toward cards with more effect keys (`cardValue` +1.5 a key, evaluation protocol 5a). The first copy reads +0.3 / −0.1 points (mirror / Map 8), so that bias is negligible at one copy. Every skill's first copy is also shown net of the placebo.
- **Opponents:** (1) the A-0 mirror. (2) The Map 8 reference deck (`WINRATE_REF_DECKS.road[8]`, level 0, so cards play as printed). A-0 wins 31.5% against it. The Map 5 and 6 decks lose 90%+ to A-0 and the Map 7 deck wins 79%, so Map 8 leaves the most room to read gains. The games are played with brambles at 30 Health (the protocol's plain bramble), no field (no Tide or Day/Night), Smart CPU on both seats and seats swapped every other game.
- **Paired games (common random numbers):** game g of an opponent has the same engine seed in every variant (`deckopt.playOne`). The deck lists the swapped slots in the same positions, so the same shuffle puts the replacement exactly where the card it replaced would have been. Differences between variants then come from the cards, not from the draw.
- **Calibration line:** 1, 2, 4 or 12 of the 12 zero-cost vanillas get +1 Attack (3 budget points each) or +2 Health (2 points each). The slope is ΔWR per budget point, fitted through the origin on the 1-4 copy bumps (the 12-copy bumps flatten near the ceiling). It comes out at 2.45 WR points a budget point in the mirror [2.11, 2.78] and 2.03 against Map 8 [1.77, 2.31]. Every "points" number below is ΔWR divided by that slope.
- **The fit:** d(n) = a(1 − e^(−n/τ)) + b·n through d(0) = 0. The part with a is the fast early gain that levels off. The b part is a late straight trend, negative when resource bodies crowd out the rest. τ is searched on a grid, a and b by least squares. A quadratic (the brief's example) misses how steep the first copies are and puts n* too early (its n* is shown beside). n* is the argmax over 0-12. Intervals come from a bootstrap over games (400 resamples). Each resample keeps a game's results together across all variants, so the pairing is respected.
- **Real cards** play at the level where their resource skill is live (Level 1, except Muskrat Lodgekeeper L2, Beaver Feller and Dust Hyena L3, Salvager Hermit Crab L6), one copy each. A card is compared three ways. **vs twin:** against a plain body of its own cost, Wait, rarity and obtain map, sized by the budget at that level. This is the price verdict: + means stronger than its budget buys. **vs slot in A-0:** in place of one 0-Lumber vanilla. **vs slot in A-n\*:** in place of one resource test creature in the A-n* deck. The cards were read in A-0 and A-n* (the brief's A-1 context was not played). For the free variants n* is 11-12, so A-n* is a deck already full of Lumber.

## The error formula, in plain words

- **One win rate:** p measured over N games has a standard error of √(p(1 − p)/N). Draws count as half, and the tool uses the spread of the 1 / ½ / 0 scores, which is the same to a hair. The 95% interval is ±1.96 standard errors. At N = 800 and p = 50% that is ±3.5 points.
- **A difference between two variants on the same games:** take the per-game difference (+1, +½, 0, −½, −1). Its standard error is the spread of those differences divided by √N, which equals √((p₁(1 − p₁) + p₂(1 − p₂) − 2·cov)/N). Pairing makes the covariance large, so the error shrinks. +1 Attack on one vanilla reads ±2.4 points paired. Unpaired it would be ±4.9 (√(2 · 0.25 / 800) × 1.96). That is a quarter of the variance, or the same precision as four times the games.
- **n\* and the gain:** the bootstrap interval described above.
- **Budget points:** ΔWR / slope. The ± shown is the ΔWR's alone. The slope adds about ±14% (its own 95% interval) on top.
- **The pricing error the owner asked about:** the target is not WR(A-n) = WR(A-0). The useful readings are the best mixture (n*) and the worth of a copy, and **price error = worth − price**. Worth is the first copy's ΔWR (A-1 over A-0, net of the placebo) divided by the slope. Price is the S the table gives the test creature. For a 1-Lumber variant the budget's expectation is S − 3.3, since the Lumber of cost buys 3.3 points of N. For a real card the price error is its ΔWR over its budget twin divided by the slope.

## Caveats

- **Two contexts only:** the mirror and Map 8, with no field. Tide, Day/Night and Nocturnal parts are switched off, which costs the Tide cards (Giant Clam, Muskrat Lodgekeeper, Coral Polyp Colony) and the Nocturnal ones (Glowworm Cluster, Trash Panda Trickster).
- **Attack and Health are not worth 3 : 1 here.** Against Map 8, +1 Attack is worth 2.58 WR points a budget point and +2 Health 0.81. In the mirror it is 2.66 and 1.96. The pooled slope sits between them, so converting with the Attack-only slope would shrink every point figure by about 5-20%, and the Health-only slope would raise it.
- **A swap is one copy.** Each surplus carries about ±1.0 points (95%) plus the slope's ±14%. A card within ±1.5 points is "not measurably off", not "fair".
- **Resource bodies here have nothing big to buy.** The test deck's Lumber sinks are eight cards costing 12 Lumber in all. A deck with costlier cards would value Lumber more, and one with only cheap cards less.
