# MAPCTX: skills measured on their own map (2026-11-21)

A new kind of skill measure. Until now every skill was measured on one shared setup (every road card, every road field, Level 1, bramble 15).
Here each skill is measured on a map where players meet it: that map's own card pool, its fights' fields and its reference level
(`tools/pipeline/mapctx.sh`; keys `docs/balance/pipeline/state/mapctx-NN.json` = the skills carried by that map's pool; results in
`measures.json` under `mapN|<key>`). Late maps' pools hold no skill-less card, so every map uses the shared setup's six carriers
(soldier-ant, glass-golem, blessed-avatar, stable-colt, rivergate-recruit, lucky-hopper) played at the map's level. Early stopping as usual
(200 / 400 / 700 / 1,000 fights; most items converged at 200).

## Confirm: the 89 close calls at 1,000 fights
Every flagged item whose gap was under half its price and that had stopped at 200 fights was played again at 1,000
(`MAPCTX_SET=mapctx-confirm MAPCTX_ARGS="--fights 1000 --force" bash tools/pipeline/mapctx.sh ...`). **86 of 89 kept their verdict, none
flipped, 3 moved into range:** Daunt 2 on Map 11 (2.47 vs 2.56), Bulldoze 1 on Map 12 (2.32 vs 2.23, so Bulldoze leaves the
"cheap everywhere" list), Trap 6 on Map 18 (3.82 vs 3.74). The 200-fight readings are saved in `mapctx-rows-2026-11-21.json`, the confirmed
ones in `mapctx-rows-2026-11-21-confirm.json`. The early stop at 200 fights is trustworthy for these verdicts.

## BSWEEP: every Maps 1-20 skill at bramble 5, 15 and 30 (127 skills)
A second new measure: the shared setup played at bramble Health 5 and 30 (the road uses 15), to see whether a skill's worth depends on how
long fights last (`run.sh measure --bramble H --ctx-tag bH`; results under `global@b5|` / `global@b30|`; table
`node tools/pipeline/bsweep_report.js` -> `bsweep-2026-11-21.json`). The first 32 (those off on every map) ran first; the other 95 after.

- **101 of 127 read the same at all three** (5 and 30 intervals overlap). 57 keep the same verdict at all three; most that move sit near
  their price.
- **Of the 32 off on every map, 20 keep their verdict at all three**: a re-price from the bramble-15 measure holds for them.
- **Worth rises in long fights (14):** weapons and slow effects. Harbinger 1 3.2 / 4.6 / 7.8 (price 6.5), Paralysis Powder 1 3.4 / 4.7 / 6.7,
  Trident -0.3 / 2.8 / 3.3, Pitchfork -1.5 / 1.2 / 2.8, Arrow 2 4.5 / 6.3 / 7.5, Wave, Carrion 2, Snipe 2, Guardian, Parasite.
- **Worth falls in long fights (12):** opening burst. Stealth 16.7 / 6.9 / 3.0 (price 6.88), Arrow 2 (bramble) 15.8 / 8.2 / 7.7, Pounce 6
  14.7 / 7.8 / 7.7, Pounce 3 8.8 / 4.6 / 3.2, Pounce 1, Siege 3, Trap 5 / 6, Dynamite 3, Offspring 1, Taunt, Draw on Attack.

So prices fit the road's fight length. Modes with shorter or longer fights (raid, co-op, PvP) would move Stealth, Pounce and the weapons most.

## CARRIER: the same 127 skills on hard hitters and on walls
A third new measure: each skill on three high-Attack carriers (glass-golem 6/5, soldier-ant 3/7, blessed-avatar 3/13) and on three
high-Health ones (lucky-hopper 1/17, rivergate-recruit 2/12, stable-colt 2/9), bramble 15 (`run.sh measure --carriers ... --ctx-tag atk|hp`;
table `node tools/pipeline/carrier_report.js` -> `carrier-2026-11-21.json`).

- **93 of 127 are worth the same on either body.**
- **Worth more on walls (20): ranged and siege.** Arrow 2 (bramble) 9.7 vs 5.3, Arrow 3 (never swings) 4.7 vs -2.3, Arrow 1 + Fletcher
  8.9 vs 5.2, Arrow 2, Fire Arrow 1, Siege 1 / 3, Dynamite 3, Poison Powder 2, Inspiring 2, Sweep 3, Electricity 2, Pack 1, Swarm, Exploit 2,
  and Taunt / Guardian (a cost on hard hitters, a small gain on walls). A body that lasts keeps shooting.
- **Worth more on hard hitters (14): survival and tempo.** Armor 3 7.3 vs 3.9, Bulwark 2 8.6 vs 5.1, Sturdy 4.0 vs 2.5, Swift 6.0 vs 3.9,
  On Death spawn 3 Worker Ants 9.4 vs 4.8, Meiosis 1, Stun on Spawn, Scare 1, Sap, Carrion 2. Protecting or speeding a big hitter pays more.

The budget prices a skill the same on any body. A body term would move these 34 (decision CARRIER-1).

A fourth new measure: the shared setup with 2 or 12 copies of the skilled card in the test deck instead of 6, bramble 15

- **119 of 127 are worth the same per copy at 2, 6 and 12** (the 2- and 12-copy intervals overlap); 61 keep the same verdict at all three.
- **Worth more with copies (2): Pack 1** 2.0 / 4.5 / 5.2 (price 3.25) and **Swarm** 1.8 / 4.0 / 4.9 (3.13): they count allies of their kind.
  At 2 copies both are too dear, at 12 too cheap.
- **Worth less with copies (6): one big effect stops paying twice.** Avalanche 1 13.0 / 9.0 / 6.3 (9.25), Twin Shot 2 9.6 / 8.5 / 6.3 (13.26),

So the budget's one-copy price holds for almost every skill; Pack and Swarm are the exceptions a deck-level rule would need.

## LEVEL: the same 127 skills with both decks at Level 1, 3 and 5 (2026-11-22)
A fifth new measure: the shared setup played at Level 3 and Level 5 (Map 20's level) instead of Level 1, bramble 15
(`run.sh measure --level L --ctx-tag LL`; table `node tools/pipeline/level_report.js` -> `level-2026-11-22.json`). The carriers grow by their
levelBonus; the measured skill keeps its printed number, so this reads whether one skill point is worth more or less on bigger bodies.
Prices barely move with level (only Arrow 3 never-swings' drawback: -4.9 -> -7.4).

- **119 of 127 read the same at Level 1 and 5** (120 at Level 3); 93 keep the same verdict.
- **Worth rises on bigger bodies (3): Attack scalers.** Stealth 6.9 / 9.6 / 10.5 (price 6.88), Mighty 4.5 / 6.0 / 6.3 (4.79), Gain Lumber on
  Spawn 4.8 / 7.1 / 7.0 (4.24). Twin Strike rises at Level 3 (6.9 -> 8.8) and sits back inside at 5.
- **Worth falls (5): fixed damage.** Pounce 1 1.9 / 1.2 / 0.4 (1.66), Pounce 3 4.6 / 4.6 / 3.5 (4.15), Arrow 2 6.3 / 6.1 / 5.0 (7.01), Fire Arrow 1
  5.9 / 5.3 / 4.9 (6.56), Arrow 3 never-swings 1.6 / -0.7 / -1.1. A fixed hit is a smaller share of a bigger body; levelBonus raising a skill's
  number at later levels (growth styles C and D) is what keeps these cards level, so they need it more than an Attack scaler does.

So one price at every level holds for nearly every skill; a growth table that raises Attack scalers' numbers would over-feed them.

## AI: the same 127 skills with both seats on the basic, smart and smartest CPU (2026-11-22)
A sixth new measure: the shared setup with both seats played by CPU level 0 (basic) or 2 (smartest) instead of 1 (smart), bramble 15
(`run.sh measure --ai A --ctx-tag aiA`; table `node tools/pipeline/ai_report.js` -> `ai-2026-11-22.json`).

- **Basic vs smart: all 127 read the same** (every interval overlaps). Across the 127 the two readings correlate 1.00 with slope 0.94; the mean
  shift is -0.10 and the mean size of a shift 0.22. The basic CPU shrinks skills slightly toward 0: plays that need timing read a little lower
  (Avalanche 1 8.1 vs 9.0, Swarm 3.2 vs 4.0, Pounce 6 7.1 vs 7.8, Strangle 2 5.2 vs 6.0, Stealth 6.2 vs 6.9) and drawbacks cost a little less
  (Parasite -2.2 vs -2.7, Purblind -2.0 vs -2.5, Recoil 1 -1.3 vs -1.8).
- 106 keep the same verdict; the 21 that flip sit at the edge of their interval (none crosses from too cheap to too dear).
- Smartest: running.

So skill prices do not depend on how well the CPU plays: the shared measure at the smart level stands for every level.

## What it means for balance
- A context skill's single price is wrong on most maps: too cheap at home, too dear away. Decision MAPCTX-1.
- 32 skills are off the same way on every map they meet: those can be re-priced once, with SKM4-1's 60 combat flags.
- Parasite (-9 to -10 on Maps 7 and 12) is the largest single miss: as a drawback it costs far more than its +1 price assumes.
- Map 18's high gap may come partly from its ranged skills being worth far more there than they cost (both sides carry them).
- Nothing here changes a card: prices only move the budget, and card stats stay until the owner asks.
