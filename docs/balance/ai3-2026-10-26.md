# AI3: the CPU's turn planner and Lumber reserve, measured (2026-10-26)

Package AI3 (action items A1 and A2, owner notes #26 and #33 / EN6-3). The engine's AI region changed (`bramblewood-engine.js`, the "AI3" blocks); no skirmish deck, Shield or Health changed (EN8-10 is open; the owner: "Don't retune yet").

## What changed

- **A2, the reserve (every CPU level).** The CPU knows its deck's average Lumber cost (the deck as dealt) and keeps a reserve of average − 1. It pitches only:
  - to afford a high-value card: a card short only of resources, worth at least 1.5× the best card it can play now, that this pitch and the next one cover (with nothing it can play, any card short of resources counts);
  - to keep the reserve: the Lumber left after its best play now would fall below it (with 3+ cards in hand, 2+ when it plays nothing);
  - with nothing to play and a full hand: the draw would burn its top card for the same 1 Lumber, so it chooses the card to burn.
  - The old EN8 rule pitched whenever it held a card it could not afford (with 3+ cards in hand), so a cheap deck pitched away cards it never needed the Lumber for. The end-of-turn fallback ("nothing played: pitch") now also needs one of the reasons above.
- **A1, the planner (Smart and Smartest).** Each playable card in each legal slot is an option, scored as the card's worth on this board plus a cheap one-ply look at the column after the play: the kill it makes now, the hit on its bramble it stops, whether it dies there, the Bloom it makes possible or spoils, a win this round. A card with no slot (a spell, equipment, a hazard, an evolute, a Parasite) is one option at its worth. The pitch is decided first, by A2, because its Lumber pays for this turn's play. The best option is played.
  - Bloom: a Bloom card whose conditions can hold (true now = 1; a day / night or tide condition that comes round = 0.45; "has killed" = 0.4) is worth its copy in a slot with a free neighbour. A slot that takes the last free neighbour of one of its own unbloomed Bloom units costs that copy.
  - The levels stay apart. Basic keeps its card-then-slot pick with its dice. Smart: 1.5 of noise on a card's worth and 0.4 on each slot. Smartest: 0.5 on a card's worth, no slot noise, the lane pull and tie rule, and its own pitch pick, field threshold, Sacrifice timing and Dark Summon aim.
  - Speed: under 5 ms a turn with five cards on a busy board (`tests/ai3-2026-10-26.js`).

## How it was measured

`node tools/skirmish_pass.js measure 40 <shard> 2 --tag=<tag> --maxmap=20` (and `--ref=ref2`): every fight of Maps 0-20 and their side maps (241 fights), 40 games each, seeds 9100 + 37g. "Before" is this branch's start, which includes ENG9. Each number is the mean of the fights' win rates for the new player, in %. "In band" counts the fights inside their road-curve band (`skirmishTargetBand`). At 40 games a single fight is about ±15 points, so read the map means, not single fights.

**The tool's reference player is the CPU too** (Smart, your seat). A1 and A2 therefore change both seats in the tool's own numbers. The "CPU only" row runs the same games with the new rules on the CPU's seat only and the old rules on yours. That row is the change a human player meets.

Files (in `docs/balance/`): `skirmish-pass-ai3-before-map.jsonl`, `skirmish-pass-ai3-after-map.jsonl`, `skirmish-pass-ai3-before-ref2.jsonl`, `skirmish-pass-ai3-after-ref2.jsonl`, `skirmish-pass-ai3-cpuonly-map.jsonl`; the trial variants are in `ai3-variants/`.

Each row changes one thing against "before". "CPU seat" rows run the new rules for the CPU only.

| A1 on the smartest slot score + A2 without saving, CPU seat (**shipped**) | 68.9 | 68.7 | +0.6 | −1.8 | `skirmish-pass-vi-map.jsonl`, `../skirmish-pass-ai3-cpuonly-map.jsonl` |

What the trials decided:
- **Saving**: a version that also held its other plays back while it gathered for the big card (it played only cards that kept the big card in reach next turn) cost the CPU about 1 point. A looser "high value" made it worse. Shipped: the gathering is the pitch alone, at 1.5× and a reach of 2.
- **The smart CPU's slot score**: its old trade check (kill 2, out-last 1) inside the planner was no better than its old dice (+0.5, noise). The smartest CPU's column score made it about 3 points stronger, so both planning levels use it.

A positive change means the player wins more (the CPU got weaker). The first-cut rows changed the reference player's seat too. The shipped row is the CPU's seat alone.
