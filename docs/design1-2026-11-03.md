# DESIGN1: is the current card design fun? (2026-11-03)

Owner 2026-11-03 18:37: "Is the current card design fun? (Design != balance and normally design comes first, but i wanted a real good balance pass first, before further design decisions were made)".

**Status: review only.** No game file changed. The owner calls are DESIGN1-1 to DESIGN1-11 in `docs/decisions.json` (status YET).

What I read: `canonical/cards.json` (906 cards; 394 in play, meaning on a map list or in a road deck of Maps 0-24, not test or token), `canonical/map-pools.json`, the road decks in `arena_app.js` (through `tools/opt/deckopt.js loadGame`), the reference ladder (`docs/balance/ref/ref-ladder.json`, 10 decks a list), the PRO / BPRO decks (`canonical/pro-skirmishes.json`, 232 optimizer decks), the skill measures (`docs/balance/pipeline/measures.json`), BAL13 to BAL15 and THEME5, `docs/project-state.md`, `docs/lore-bible.md`.

How I counted:
- A **skill** is an effect key or a trigger. Flying, Tide, Nocturnal and Diurnal are not counted (the two-skill rule does not count them either).
- A card is **picked** when the ladder or a PRO / BPRO deck plays it. Those are the optimizer's decks: the closest thing we have to "what a good player plays".
- The read-only scripts are in the scratchpad, not the repo. Section 5 has the method for each number.

## 1. Verdict

**The game has good bones but plays flat.** Lanes, Wait, the bramble race and map fields are a sound frame. A core of cards gives real decisions (section 3 lists 11 of them). Most of the roster does not, for four reasons:

- **Almost no deck plays the expensive cards.** The strongest ladder decks are 61% 0-Lumber cards and 98% 0-1 Lumber cards.
- **The bodies repeat.** 61 in-play cards have no skill, and 70% of the in-play cards have one skill or none. Six cards are the same 0-Lumber, Wait-0, 2/7 with no skill.
- **Positional skills don't pay.** Guardian, Taunt and Crab Claws measure about zero. Damage that skips the lane fight measures highest.
- **How you play barely shows in the sims.** Over 10 maps, the player seat at the smart CPU level beats the basic level by 5.7 points on average, and the smartest level is no better than smart (section 5). The deck decides fights more than the play does.

**Maps 0-1 are the weakest part.** Their enemy decks are 82% and 70% vanilla, and Thistlewood is won 97.5% of the time (+29 over target). The tutorial's T-2 to T-4 show more interesting cards than the first two maps do.

**The fix is not more keywords.** 173 distinct effects appear by Map 24, and 24 of 25 maps break the "two new skills a map" rule. The fixes are:
- fewer and sharper verbs on cards;
- a reason to play a 2-3 Lumber card;
- positional skills that matter;
- leaders, brambles and Rares that feel like what they are.

## 2. The top 10 design problems, ranked by impact on fun

Each problem has one small, testable design change, a rule or a card-text change rather than a stat re-tune, and the measure that would show it worked.

### 1. The Lumber curve has collapsed: one free card a turn is always the play

The evidence:
- Map lists hold 0 / 1 / 2 / 3+ Lumber cards at 38 / 46 / 12 / 4%.
- The strongest decks hold them at:
  - ladder ranks 8-10: 61 / 37 / 2 / 0%;
  - PRO / BPRO decks: 55 / 42 / 3 / 1%.
- On Maps 0-20, the share of list cards no optimizer deck ever picks rises with cost: 0 Lumber 4%, 1 Lumber 8%, 2 Lumber 15%, 3 Lumber 45%.
- Dead examples: Orca Vanguard 3L 7/7 Sweep, Magma Titan 3L, Yeti 3L 5/29 Scare, Riverworks Bastion 3L 0/31 Rampart, Deep Cave Troll 3L, Vampire Roost 2L, Stone Turtle Warden 2L.

Why: you play one card a turn and pitch one. A 2-Lumber card costs a play plus one or two pitched cards, all for one body. A 0-Lumber 2/7 costs one card. The budget prices Lumber (perLumber 2.5), but it cannot price the cards in hand you give up. The owner's rule (SK2b) is "costly cards are strong because they need an economy". Today there is nothing to spend an economy on: the top-used Lumber makers (Nutria Reedcutter, Leafcutter Gardener, Talus Pika, Driftwood Crab) sit in decks that are 98% 0-1 Lumber.

The change (DESIGN1-1): **"Big entrance."** When you play a card that costs 2 or more Lumber, you may also play one 0-Lumber card that turn. The two alternatives are in the decision.

Test:
- deckopt BPRO on Maps 5, 10, 15 and 20, before and after;
- the 2+ Lumber share of the best decks should move from 2-3% toward the lists' 16%;
- the 45% dead rate of 3-Lumber cards should fall;
- the road totals should not move more than BAL13's noise.

### 2. Vanilla and same-shape bodies: a card is often just a number

The evidence:
- **61 in-play cards (15%) have no skill, and 215 (55%) have one.**
- Cards that share an exact shape (cost, Wait, stats and skills):
  - Chipmunk Skirmisher, Terrier Pup, Cricket Drummer, Field Mouse, Dormouse and Bramble Sprout: 0 Lumber, Wait 0, 2/7, no skill;
  - Cottontail, Otter Kit, Meadow Frog and Pond Trout: 2/5;
  - Pond Duck, House Cat and Otter Paddler: 3/3;
  - Duck Paddler, River Carp and Burrow Rabbit: Wait 1, 2/10.
- Vanilla share of the list's cards / of the ladder's copies: Map 0 65% / 65%; Map 1 47% / 56%.
- Thistlewood's five road fights are 2/7 walls under five names, plus Field Vole's Swarm. In the Thistle Baron elite, 15 of the 20 copies are vanilla (2/7, 1/10, 2/5, 3/2); the rest are four Hedgerow Shrews (Quick) and one Badger Lumberjack.
- `docs/workflows/card-data.md` line 15 says "Early rewards are vanilla (no skills)". That rule is part of the cause.

The change (DESIGN1-2):
- Drop "early rewards are vanilla".
- Give every card on the Map 0-1 lists one job, each a keyword the road already teaches by Map 3. The six 2/7s become six different cards:
  - Cricket Drummer: Inspiring 1;
  - Bramble Sprout: Thorns 1;
  - Chipmunk Skirmisher: Quick;
  - Terrier Pup: Pounce 1;
  - Dormouse: Guardian;
  - Field Mouse: On Death, gain 1 Lumber.
- The budget re-fits their stats; the shapes are not hand-tuned.

Test:
- ladder vanilla share on Maps 0-1 below 30%;
- the "skills stripped" gap (section 5) grows on Maps 0-1;
- a playtest of Maps 0-1.

### 3. The first hour: Maps 0-1 ask almost nothing of the player

The evidence:
- Road results, with your leader:
  - Map 0: 68% (+1);
  - Map 1: 97.5% (+29), 0 of 5 fights in the green band, the Thistle Baron 100% against a target of 40;
  - Map 1-A Clovermead: two fights 22-35 too hard, three fine.
- Map 0's four enemy decks are 82% vanilla copies: Pond Patrol, Thicket Swarm, Raccoon Heist and Frost Vanguard are the same bodies reshuffled.
- In the sims, the player seat at the basic and smart CPU levels wins 70% and 74% on Map 1: the decisions barely move the result (section 5).
- The richer cards appear in the tutorial come-back fights and then nowhere on the road. Rockslide Ram (On Attack buff), Dynamite Packrat, Scree Goat (Landslide), Eagle Sharpshooter (Arrow), Thornwood Palisade (Counterattack) and Toucan Courier are on no ladder or PRO deck.

The change (DESIGN1-3): **every Map 0-2 elite or boss has one visible gimmick**, a rule the player has to answer and that teaches the map's headline idea. Examples:
- The Thistle Baron: a Guardian row behind Thorns, so you learn to aim around it;
- The Old Shell: Armor 3, so Pierce matters;
- 0-4 Frost Vanguard: built around its four Quick Feral Tomcats, so playing first matters.

Also, Map 0's four fights each put one keyword in the spotlight ("see it", `docs/mechanics-onramp-2026-10-11.md`), instead of four reshuffles.

Test:
- Map 1's road gap within 10;
- the basic vs smart player-seat gap on Maps 0-2 grows (decisions start to matter);
- a playtest.

### 4. Positional skills don't pay; off-board damage is always right

The evidence (the pipeline's skill measures, `docs/balance/pipeline/measures.json`, global context, worth in stat points with the interval where it is wide):

| Skill | Worth | Cards |
|---|---|---|
| Guardian | -0.36 [-0.76, -0.01] | 18 in play (the Otter Legion's identity keyword) |
| Taunt | -0.74 | 2 in play |
| Crab Claws | -0.17 | 4 in play (on every Pebble Beach fight) |
| Hibernate | -0.72 | |
| Pack Leader | -0.32 | |
| Fireball 15 | 9.9 | |
| Volley 2 | 9.0 | |
| On Turn: spawn an Ant Scout | 8.7 | |
| Tsunami 2 | 8.4 | |
| Brood 2 | 8.3 | |
| Ice Arrow 2 | 8.1 | |
| Fire Arrow 2 | 8.0 | |

Every one of the top twelve is damage or bodies that skip the lane fight. The skills that ask "where do I stand" (Guardian, Taunt, Crab Claws, Cower, Rampart) are noise. The likely cause is in the rules (GD-3, EN8-1): Guardian redirects plain swings and counters only, while the damage that matters comes from shots and skills.

The change (DESIGN1-4): **Guardian also takes Arrows and shots aimed at its adjacent allies**, not damage skills or area damage. Taunt also pulls Arrows. This reverses part of GD-3, which is why it is an owner call.

Test: the pipeline re-measure of Guardian and Taunt (`tools/pipeline/run.sh`) should move them clearly above zero; the Otter / armor decks' BPRO share should rise.

### 5. Counterplay: the hardest fights are stat walls with no answer in the pool

The evidence:
- The worst "too hard" fights are walls:
  - 9-7 The Kelp Witch, 5% (target 40): Mangrove Manatee 7/7 Regen 2 Guardian, five Mangrove Monitors 3/12, Horseshoe Crab Armor 2, Proboscis Monkey Taunt, Reef Shark 6/13;
  - 17-7 Alpha Howler's Den, 10% (target 60): four Glacial Ape-Kings 6/40 with Earthquake 4 and Guardian;
  - 14-A-1 Dart Frogs, 2.5% (target 90): five Hoopoe Heralds 2/12, five Weaver Birds with Tangle.
- BAL13's card flags can only offer stat steps. In 20 of the 65 flagged fights, three steps on the fight's biggest enemy card are "not enough alone".
- The player's lists through Map 10 hold almost no answer to one big body. Executioner arrives on Map 14 and Freeze on Map 17. Wither (Map 7: no healing while it lasts) does answer Regen, but few optimizer decks pick it.

The change (DESIGN1-5): **an answer rule.** When a map's elite or boss leans on a threat class, that map's list (or the one before) holds at least one card that answers it:
- Armor 2+: Pierce or Venomous;
- Regen: Wither;
- 25+ Health: Executioner or Hunter;
- a Flying swarm: Reach or Arrow;
- a Taunt wall: Backstab or Arrow.

A read-only checker lists the gaps per map, for the owner's card picks (THEME5-1 can take them).

Test: the checker reads zero gaps; on those fights, the spread between the weakest and the strongest ladder deck shrinks, because answers exist and not only bigger stats.

### 6. Keyword overload and synonyms

The evidence:
- Counting a skill when it first appears in a main map's list or road decks, after the tutorial's 14, new skills a map are:
  - Map 2: 11;
  - Map 6: 9;
  - Map 7: 9;
  - Map 8: 12;
  - Map 17: 10;
  - Map 19: 10.
- The mean is 6.4 a map. Only Map 5 keeps to the settled "two new skills a map at most" (rules 10-21 RP). That is 173 distinct effects by Map 24.
- Synonyms a player cannot tell apart from the name:
  - "Lumber when Ready" is written three ways with two timings: Stockpile (once, 2 in-play cards), `onReadyGold` (every Ready round, 7) and an On Ready gain trigger (6).
  - Poison / Venomous / Greater Poison / Poison Bolt.
  - Hunter plus Hunter-vs (two keys for one skill).
  - Shell / Shield / Armor / Bulwark.
  - Crab Claws / Fork / Trident (side hits).

The change (DESIGN1-6):
- **A keyword-intro test** that counts new skills per map and fails above the rule.
- **Merge the "Lumber when Ready" family** into one keyword with a timing word: Stockpile N (once) and Stockpile N, every turn.
- Do the same for the other groups over time.

Test: the counter (it reads 24 of 25 maps over today); readability in a playtest.

### 7. Archetypes are deck shapes, not synergies

The evidence:
- Of the 232 PRO / BPRO decks, the optimizer's focus is open 71, swarm 51, economy 45, big 37 and family 28: flying 8, poison 5, tide 5, evasion 4, armor 2, fire 2, strike-first 1, cold 1.
- Only 12% are built around a type or keyword, and those are focus runs the tool was told to try.
- Payoff cards that name another card's type or keyword are rare in play:
  - Kinship: 0 (the Honeyjoe Brewer is not on a list yet);
  - Swarm by Type: 0 (plain Swarm, any shared type, is on 9);
  - Fletcher: 1 (Eagle-Owl Quartermaster);
  - Inspiring: 5.
- THEME5's 34 archetype plans each depend on a carried card: the map's own list does not hold the archetype.
- Field effects show the same thing. On the water and wind maps, 30-36% of the ladder's copies react to the field (Tide, Dive, Flying). On Maps 5-7 and 12-16 it is 1-17%: Swamp Fever, the Spore Cloud, Grasping Briars, the Canopy, Tall Grass and the Taiga's cold mostly happen to both sides alike, and no card asks you to play into them. By this count, Savanna Reaches has no field-aware card. Day / night runs on almost every map, but each list holds only 0-3 day / night cards.

The change (DESIGN1-7): **each main map gets one payoff card and one field card.**
- The payoff names a type or keyword already on its list. Examples: Pebble Beach, Kinship – Crab +1/+0; The Morel Hollow, a card that gains Attack for each Poisoned or Decaying enemy.
- The field card's skill keys off the map's field. Examples: Tall Grass, Stealth in the grass; the Taiga, a Nocturnal hunter (THEME5's Owl Nightwatch is one).
- These are card-text changes to cards already on the list, chosen from THEME5-1's candidates where possible.

Test: deckopt with a family focus on each map; the family deck lands within 5 points of the best open deck.

### 8. Rarity doesn't feel special

The evidence:
- Mean skills a card: Common 1.09, Uncommon 1.27, Rare 1.25, Very Rare 1.44. Epic is 2.86, but only 7 Epics are in play.
- The budget gives a rarity step +1 on a base of 10 (`skill-costs.json budget.perRarity`), so a Rare is a slightly bigger Common.
- **12 in-play Rare+ cards have no skill:**
  - Rare: Squire's Mount 4/14, Bramblewood Lynx 5/7, Glass Golem 6/5, Sergeant Bramblefox 3/12, Quillguard 2/22, Badger Berserker 4/16, Dolphin Knight 3/9 Tide, Pike Lancer 3/9 Tide, Raven Scout 2/5 Flying, Ashwing Ptarmigan 3/6 Flying;
  - Very Rare: Woodland Brawler 3/14, Tide Spirit 5/5 Tide.
- A pack reveal of a Rare 4/14 with no text is the moment the game should feel generous, and it doesn't.

The change (DESIGN1-8): **a signature rule.** Every Rare+ card carries one effect no Common on its first map has: a named variant, an On Play, or a two-skill combo. Start with these 12.

Test: a checker over `cards.json`; the pick rate of the 12 in the ladder (today Squire's Mount and Bramblewood Lynx are picked; Pike Lancer and Ashwing Ptarmigan are on no optimizer deck).

### 9. Leaders and brambles are flat knobs

The evidence:
- **Leaders.**
  - There are 13 leader cards. Most are a body with one or two common skills:
    - Old Shell: Armor 3;
    - Pride Queen: Quick;
    - Yeti Chieftain: Rage;
    - Old Snapjaw: Bleed 1 and Armor 1;
    - Wandering Traveller: no skill in `cards.json` beyond `leader`.
  - **Old Snapjaw is the leader your seat holds on 16 of the 36 ladder lists** (BAL13's "strongest card that may lead"). The leader you hold does not change with the map's theme.
  - A leader adds one decision: when to summon it, since it uses your play.
- **Brambles.** There are 43 brambles with 12 passive kinds, every number 1 or 2: start Lumber, first unit +1 Attack, units +1 Health, Regen 1, enemy Wait +1, bolt 1-2, Thorns 1 and the rest. Most change no decision: you pick the biggest Health.

The change (DESIGN1-9): **a Command for each leader**, a passive that works only while the leader waits in the 👑 slot and ends when you summon it. Examples:
- Old Shell: your first Guardian each fight has Armor +1;
- The Gannet Queen: your Flyers' Pounce +1;
- Old Snapjaw: enemy units that take Bleed get +1 Wait once.

That turns "summon it the first turn you can" into a real choice.

DESIGN1-10 asks whether brambles get one once-a-fight active: you tap it and choose a column. For example, Ballista Keep fires its bolt at the column you choose; Thorn Cage puts its Thorns on one column for a round.

Test:
- with Command: leader diversity on the ladder (16 of 36 today), and the mean summon turn moving off "as soon as it can be paid";
- with the bramble active: the bramble pick in the deck editor stops being "the biggest Health".

### 10. The Baby / Evomax line promises and doesn't pay

The evidence:
- The Baby cards are vanilla small bodies: Otter Kit 2/5, Duckling 1/5, Fox Kit 2/9, Sparrow Chick 1/3 Flying, Compost Grub 1/10, Snapper Hatchling 1/7 Tide, Rabbit Kit 2/2 Evasive.
- Their Hatchling skill shows "?" until Map 15 and the name only until Map 20 (`SKILL_REVEAL_MAP`). It does something only when an Evolute lands on it, and Evomax cards are obtainable from Map 30 (EV gate).
- The one Evolute card on a list, Gale Sprite (Map 21), is on no deck at all; Canopy Sloth Guardian is on no list. Evolute measures -6.41 [-7.73, -5.41] on the canon carriers.
- So a player carries "?" text on their first cards for 20-30 maps.

The change (DESIGN1-11): **Grow N.** A Baby that survives N of its own rounds gets +1/+1 once. That gives you a reason to protect it on Map 0, and it stays a Stem for Evolutes later. Alternative: hide Hatchling entirely until Map 20 rather than show "?".

Test: the pipeline price of Grow 2; Baby pick rates on Maps 0-5 (Sparrow Chick 52 ladder copies, Rabbit Kit 0 today).

## 3. Cards that are fun, and the pattern to copy

These are picked widely by the optimizer and each makes a decision.

| Card | Line | Picked | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|

| Badger Lumberjack | Uncommon, 1L 1/11, Lumberjack + Guardian | 13 lists | One of the few cards that changes your Lumber plan. |

**The pattern:** one verb you can read in a line, that reacts to the board (adjacency, a kill, a death, the round it lands) and asks "when" or "where". Two-skill cards work when the skills multiply, as with Bleed and Twin Strike or Arrow and Fletcher, rather than add.

## 5. Method and the simulated play

**Counts** come from `canonical/cards.json`, `canonical/map-pools.json`, the road decks, `docs/balance/ref/ref-ladder.json` and `canonical/pro-skirmishes.json`:
- "In play": on a list or in a road deck of Maps 0-24.
- "Dead": on a Maps 0-20 list and on no ladder or PRO / BPRO deck. That is 27 of 321. Maps 21-24 have no ladder or PRO decks, so they are left out.
- "New skills a map": first seen on a main map's list or road decks, after the tutorial's 14.

**The sims** were read-only and used the game's own harness (`deckopt loadGame`, `referenceFightSetup`, `dealPlayerLeader`). Each map's rank-5 ladder deck, with that map's leader, played every road fight of the map. Six games a fight, ten on Maps 0-1: a small sample on a busy machine (load 30-50 on 2 cores). The deck ran four ways:
- **smart:** the player seat at CPU level 1, as every balance tool plays it;
- **basic:** the player seat at level 0;
- **smartest:** the player seat at level 2;
- **vanilla:** smart, with every skill stripped from the player's cards (Tide kept, the leader kept).

| Map | Rank-5 ladder deck | Games a fight | Smart | Basic | Smartest | Skills stripped | Smart - basic | Smart - stripped |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Bramblewood Outskirts | Bee Sentry Hive | 10 | 57.5 | 40.0 | 55.0 | 35.0 | +17.5 | +22.5 |
| 1 Thistlewood | Honey Bee Hive | 10 | 74.0 | 70.0 | 74.0 | 26.0 | +4.0 | +48.0 |
| 2 Pebble Beach | Grey Seal Bull School | 6 | 43.3 | 43.3 | 43.3 | 13.3 | +0.0 | +30.0 |
| 3 Gannet Cliffs | Bee Sentry Swarm | 6 | 33.3 | 23.8 | 23.8 | 19.0 | +9.5 | +14.3 |
| 5 Gatorback Swamp | Peak Condor Swarm | 6 | 50.0 | 45.2 | 54.8 | 54.8 | +4.8 | -4.8 |
| 8 Caves & Alcoves | Poison Dart Croaker Brood | 6 | 83.3 | 72.9 | 85.4 | 62.5 | +10.4 | +20.8 |
| 11 Eyrie Heights | Booming Bittern Flock | 6 | 45.0 | 30.0 | 41.7 | 15.0 | +15.0 | +30.0 |
| 14 Savanna Reaches | Hawthorn Shieldbug Swarm | 6 | 66.7 | 64.6 | 60.4 | 54.2 | +2.1 | +12.5 |
| 17 Wolfsbane Tundra | Lovebird Swarm | 6 | 33.3 | 45.8 | 41.7 | 10.4 | -12.5 | +22.9 |
| 20 The Sundered Peak | River Carp Swarm | 6 | 25.0 | 18.3 | 28.3 | 11.7 | +6.7 | +13.3 |
| **Mean of 10 maps** | | | 51.1 | 45.4 | 50.8 | 30.2 | +5.7 | +21.0 |

What it shows:
- **The play policy moves little.** The smart seat beats the basic one by 5.7 points on average, from -12.5 on Map 17 to +17.5 on Map 0. The smartest seat is no better than the smart one (50.8 vs 51.1). Within this sample, how the AI picks its card and slot is worth a few points, and which cards are in the deck is worth far more. A human plays differently from any AI level, so this is a hint, not proof.
- **The skills carry about 21 points, but not evenly.**
  - Map 2 (Grey Seal Bull School) and Map 11: stripping the skills costs 30 points.
  - Map 14: it costs 12.
  - Map 5 (Peak Condor Swarm): it costs nothing (54.8 stripped against 50 smart). That deck's skills are Reload (a drawback) with Flying on the Condor, Guardian, Armor 2, Poison 1 with Swarm, and a conditional Bloom: mostly the skills problem 4 measures at about zero.
- A fight lasts about 6-14 rounds with one play a turn, so it holds roughly 6-14 decisions.

How to read it:
- smart vs basic and smartest is a rough measure of how much the choice of card and slot matters. When the three levels win about the same, the decisions the AI levels differ on (slot choice, trades, when to pitch) do not move fights.
- smart vs vanilla is how much the skills carry the deck.

The sample is small, so read a gap under about 15 points as noise.
