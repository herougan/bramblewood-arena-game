## 4. Living card art and the "3D card"

### What exists
Card art is one opaque 256x256 webp per card (895 files, 15 MB). There is no alpha and no layers, so nothing in the art can move. The Detail card already tilts and glares; that is a flat picture on a card, not depth.

### Plan: living card art (ART17), layered, premium only
Quality gate: if the layer split looks cut-out or has fringes, stop. No rectangles drawn in code; all pixels come from the generator.
1. Split each chosen card into 3 to 4 aligned layers, all 512x512 (art is 256 today; the Detail card shows it 400 px wide, so 512 gives sharp art) webp with alpha: `bg` (sky, water or ground, painted behind the animal, full frame), `body` (the animal, alpha mask), `eyes` (just the iris and glow, alpha), `fx` (stars, sparks, light shafts, snow, firefly dots, alpha). How to get them from PixelLab: generate the background-only image first (prompt: the scene without the creature), then the creature on that background as today; get the body mask by differencing / segmenting (rembg-style matting, then a hand check), take the eye layer by painting out a small region with the matte and keeping a soft-edged crop; ask the generator for the "fx" layer on a transparent / flat black background and key it by luminance (additive blend). Account note: PixelLab subscription shows inactive (action-items ART16), so this step is blocked on credits the owner decides on; local matting needs no credit.
2. Runtime: a `<canvas>` is not needed. Stack the layers as `<img>` in the Detail card only: `bg`, `body`, `eyes`, `fx` at different `translateZ` / parallax multipliers (bg 1x, body 2x, fx 3x of the tilt) driven by the existing `--ctTiltX/Y` (pointer) and gyro on the phone. Eyes: opacity pulse and a slow glint (a gradient mask sliding across the eye layer; CSS `mask-position`). Twinkle: the `fx` layer with `steps()` opacity on two copies, or 6 to 10 tiny sprite stars from the atlas with staggered `animation-delay` and `opacity` / `scale` only. Day-dusk: two painted `bg` variants cross-faded by the game's clock (the weather / day cycle already exists for maps) plus a colour grade on `body` through a pre-baked second webp, not a CSS `filter` (filter costs a layer per card). Seasons: the `bg` variants again (4 files), chosen by the map season (UIFX2 already has the season).
3. Where it runs: the Detail card, the pack reveal for rare and up, the hero plinth. Never on board or hand tiles (they keep the flat art; the guardrail against per-card layers stays). One card animating at a time; paused when `ui27Away()`, reduced motion and Effects Low show the flat art.
4. Budget: 4 layers at about 25-40 KB = 100-160 KB a card, loaded on demand when the Detail card opens; 40 hero cards = ~5 MB, not in the first load. GPU memory: 4 x 512x512 = 4 MB, one card at a time.

### One-card feasibility step (about half a day, no game file changed until it passes)
Pick Abyss Lantern Squid (eyes, glowing lure, dark water: the best case for glow and a drifting fx layer). (a) Re-generate or matte its `bg` and `body`; check the edge on light and dark backgrounds at 400 px. (b) Cut the eye and lure layer. (c) Prototype as an Effects Lab entry only (the Lab already loads one card), not in the game. (d) Measure: CPU and frames on the Detail card at 1x / 4x with `tools/perf` (target: under 2 ms script per frame, one extra compositor layer, no style pass per frame), and screenshots at 390 and 1366 with `tools/shot.py`. (e) The owner looks at the screenshots and a 3 s clip. Go / no-go: if the matte shows fringing or the eye layer looks pasted on, report "not premium" and use the fallback below.

Fallback method (if the split is not clean): do not animate the art; animate the card around it with 2D shader passes (a heat-haze or water ripple masked by luminance, as `bramblewood-shaders.js` scene mode already does for the splash art: green sways, blue ripples, bright blooms). That reads the picture's own pixels, so no mask art is needed, but it only moves what the colour tells it to.

### A "3D card" at 60 fps on a phone
- One card is 3D at a time (the one in the hand of the player: Detail card, pack reveal, hero). Board and hand tiles stay flat with `box-shadow`.
- Tilt: `transform` only (rotateX/Y from CSS variables written once per frame by one rAF loop, `will-change: transform` while handled, removed after), no layout reads in the loop (cache the rect on `pointerenter`, listen once on the stage).
- Depth: 3 to 4 `translateZ` layers (section above) with `transform-style: preserve-3d` on the card only; no `filter`, no full-screen blend layer. Foil sheen: a pre-rendered gradient texture whose `background-position` shifts with tilt, in the existing `.dc-glare` (soft-light, only while handled). Light edge: a thin rim gradient that follows the tilt.
- Phone input: `deviceorientation` (iOS needs a permission tap, so the first tilt is a tap on the card), low-passed, +-12 degrees, off when hidden.
- Optional high end: a WebGL depth-displacement card (one canvas for the Detail card only, a depth map generated offline per card, a fragment shader shifting the colour map by depth and tilt, plus the foil as a real specular). Real depth, no layer seams, one draw call; render at device pixel ratio capped at 2 and 30 fps ambient / 60 while tilting. Needs a depth map per card (monocular depth model, offline), so it fits the same 40 hero cards, and falls back to the CSS layers.
- Guard: measure with `tools/perf` before release; the card must hold 16.7 ms a frame on the 4x CPU run, and Effects Low shows the flat card.
