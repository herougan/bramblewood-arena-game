# ECON2: economy review for alpha (2026-11-03)

Owner 2026-11-03 18:37: "Quest and reward and trail rewards balancing. How much dialogue happens. Where to place objects on the map etc. Game economy balancing basically".

The balancing roadmap (`docs/workflows/balance.md`) puts skirmish rewards and pack cards after deck and map balancing. So this package **measures and proposes only. It changes no game number.** The owner calls are ECON2-1 to ECON2-12 in `docs/decisions.json`.

Every number below comes from a run in this session:
- `node tools/econ2/econ_sim_2026_11_03.js` writes `docs/balance/econ2-sim-2026-11-03.json`. It plays the economy day by day using the game's own tables. It reads them out of `arena_app.js` with ECON1's halving applied the way the source applies it.
- `python3 tools/econ2/map_probe_2026_11_03.py` takes a local build in a headless browser and writes the screenshots in `docs/screens/econ2/` plus `docs/balance/econ2-maps-2026-11-03.json`.
- `python3 tools/econ2/map_report_2026_11_03.py` writes `docs/balance/econ2-maps-report-2026-11-03.json`.

## Summary

- **The road has a cliff at Map 11.**
  - Maps 0-10 take a dedicated player 3 days (a casual player 7). Nothing on them costs anything, so time or Energy is the only limit.
  - From Map 11 the Forge opens and **Magic Dust becomes the only thing that limits progress.**
  - Suppose the player keeps the deck at the level the road curve is tuned for (`referencePlayerLevel`) and uses each map's reference cards. Then Maps 11-20 take **46 days (casual 66)**, and 45 of those days are spent waiting for Dust.
  - A player who keeps one deck from Map 11 onward needs only 5.8k Dust in all. That player finishes Map 20 on **day 10 (casual 20)**. Real players will sit between these two bounds.
- **Flooded currencies at Map 20 (dedicated player):**
  - Maple Leaves: 12.1k held while Dust is at 0.6k.
  - Kibble 362, Iron Shards 188, Metal 45 (nothing spends Metal).
- **Starved:**
  - Dust, from Map 11 on.
  - Energy, for a two-session player from Map 5. They get 57-107 Energy a day out of a possible 288, because the cap is 20-50.
  - Gold Leaves arrive at about 1 a day. This is scarce by design, but it is an open call (ECON2-12).
- **Two quest-board quirks drive the numbers:**
  - Weekly quests pay their tier's reward **for each quest**, not once per tier. That is about 40% of a dedicated player's Dust after the road.
  - Weekly tier 1 holds a PvP quest, so tiers 2-3 stay hidden from Map 4 until the Arena opens on Map 11.
- **Rewards that don't scale with effort:**
  - A repeat skirmish pays 4 Leaves and 0 Dust on every map. That is 4 Leaves an Energy on Map 0 and 0.44 on Map 20.
  - First clears pay the same 40 / 20 per skirmish on every map. The road's pay per Energy falls from 13.9 Leaves on Map 0 to 3.6 on Map 20.
  - Errands pay 14-27 Dust, less than a D notice.
- **Dialogue is front-loaded:**
  - The whole game holds 62 NPC scripts: 171 lines, 1,884 words.
  - The first hour (tutorial and Maps 0-2) plays about 13 NPC conversations (about 40 lines), plus the tutorial's own coach tips.
  - From Map 15 most maps have one conversation or none. Boss banter is one generic two-line exchange, and it fires on only 7 main-road fights, because most map enders are elites.
- **Map objects:**
  - On **20 maps a fight node sits under the map's title panel**: 15 main maps and 5 side maps, most often fight X-2 (for example 3-1, 5-2, 20-2 and 20-3).
  - Most maps from 3 to 20 use the same perimeter loop: across the top, down the right, back along the bottom. That leaves the centre empty.
  - 13 pairs of tap targets sit closer than 7% of the canvas. The worst is 8-5 and 8-H at 2.8% on the dark Caves map.

## 1. A new player's progression, simulated

### The model

The model follows a new player from Map 0 through the end of Map 20, one day at a time. **Expected values, not dice:** a fight won with chance p costs 1/p tries, and each try costs its Energy.

The game's numbers it uses:

| What | Source in the code |
|---|---|
| Energy per fight | `nodeEnergyCost` |
| Win chance per road fight | `roadCurveTarget`: the curve's target, not the measured rate (see the caveat) |
| First-clear and repeat pay | `nodeRewardTier`, halved |
| Notices | `QUEST_RANK_REWARD` / `_WEIGHT`, halved |
| Weekly quests | `QUEST_TIERS.weekly`, halved; paid per quest, as `claimQuest` pays |
| Feature openings (notices Map 4, quest board Map 8, Arena and Forge Map 11, Raid Map 13) | `FEATURE_SPOTS` map numbers |
| Level track | `RES3_LEVEL_TRACK` and `xpToNext` |
| The Trail Mix Trek | its point and tier tables |
| Map-end packs | `MAP_FREE_PACKS` |
| Materia from first clears | `rollMateriaDrops` |
| Forge steps and tiers | `canonical/upgrade-costs.json` |
| Max Energy steps | `STAMINA_PLACES` |
| Nest slots | `NEST_UNLOCKS` |

**Spending order:**
1. From Map 11 the player keeps the deck at `referencePlayerLevel(map)`, the level the road curve is tuned for. This is the reference deck of each map at its level mix: 3 on Maps 11-15, 3.18 on Map 16, 3.9 on Map 20.
2. Before a map's first fight, the player waits and farms until they can pay for that level.
3. Forge tiers, Max Energy and nest slots come after the deck.

**Two players:**
- **Dedicated:** checks in all day, so Energy never sits at the cap. Plays about 2 hours of fights a day, does 6 notices a day, all weekly quests, 10 PvP and 1 raid.
- **Casual:** 2 sessions of 30 minutes a day, 3 notices, all weekly quests it can reach, 3 PvP.

A fight takes 3 minutes. Repeat wins are farmed at rank S.

**Three variants:**
- **keep one deck:** the Map 11 reference deck levelled to each map's level. This is the lower bound of the Forge bill.
- **casual keep one deck.**
- **a weekly tier paid once.**

### Where the money comes from and goes

To the end of Map 20, dedicated player:

| | First clears | Notices | Weekly quests | Repeat wins | Level track | Errands | Trail | PvP + raids | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ✨ Dust | 7,294 | 11,919 | 9,767 | 3,986 | 1,050 | 895 | 770 | 226 | 35.9k |
| 🍁 Leaves | 8,670 | 10,349 | 6,377 | 9,263 | 2,100 | 565 | 730 | 879 | 38.9k |

Spent: the Forge took 30.7k Dust and 20.4k Leaves, Forge tiers 1.1k / 2.1k, Max Energy 2.5k Dust, nest slots 0.8k / 3.5k.

**Starved:**
- **Magic Dust, Maps 11-20.** After the first clears, a dedicated player's Dust is notices (243 a day) plus weekly quests (about 210 a day from Map 13). The road asks for 3.8k Dust before Map 15 and 6.6k before Map 20, because each map's new reference cards start at Level 1. The cliff is sharp:
  - Map 10 is reached on day 3 with 2.5k Dust in the bank.
  - Map 11 already asks the player to wait 2 days (casual: 0, because its slower road banked more notices).
  - **Murgle Mayhem** (ECON1: 759 Dust a week for exactly this grind) is built but hidden (`DAILY_DUNGEONS_HIDDEN = true`). Players get none of it today.
- **Energy, for a casual player from Map 5.**
  - Two sessions a day give twice the cap plus about 12: 57 Energy at cap 20, 77 at 30, 107 at 50.
  - A Map 5 fight costs 5 Energy and a Map 10 fight costs 7. So from Map 5 the casual player runs out of Energy before running out of time: on 6 of 6 days on Maps 5-10.
  - A dedicated player is never Energy-bound before Map 21.
- **Gold Leaves:** about 1 a day (dedicated) or 0.5 (casual), from S+ notices only. 51 by the end of Map 20. A Tidemire Chest costs 6 and a Highcrest Case 11, each with its Leaves.

**Flooded:**
- **Maple Leaves:**
  - Income runs at about the same pace as Dust (38.9k against 35.9k).
  - The Forge takes Leaves at 2/3 of its Dust, so Leaves pile up: 12.1k at Map 20. That would buy 240 Hedgerow Pouches.
  - Packs are the only big sink. The sim buys none, so in real play some of this goes to packs.
- **Kibble:** 362 by Map 20. Nest training costs 2-8 a card, and eggs and babies, not Kibble, set the pace.
- **Iron Shards:** 188 by Map 20, because a repeat win drops one 15% of the time. The Max Energy steps use 7, and the Tavern and Blacksmith only a few.
- **Metal:** 45-85 by Map 20, from weekly quests and the Trail. Nothing spends Metal.
- **Road progress, Maps 0-10:** 3 days for a dedicated player, all of it limited by play time and none by the economy. The first 11 maps are a sprint before the Forge grind.

**Caveat: win rates.** The sim plays each fight at the road curve's target win rate. The measured rates are off on many maps: BAL15 has 80 of 226 road fights in band and 65 beyond 20 points. For example, Thistlewood plays at 97.5% against a target of 68. Easier fights make the road faster still, but they do not change the Dust cliff.

**Outliers:**
- **Map 20 costs 269 Energy with its losses,** more than Maps 0-7 together, at the deepest point of the Dust cliff.
- **Map 11 costs 173,** half again more than Map 10. Its 11 fights include many low-target ones.

**Too much:**
- **Maps 0-2 and 8 and 11.** Map 11 opens three features at once, each with its conversation, plus the Forge golem. That is the busiest map after Map 0, and it lands exactly where the Forge grind starts.
- **Errand offers.** From Map 8, after 30% of wins, each a 2-3 line conversation for 14-27 Dust (section 2). A dedicated player gets about 2 offers a day: the most frequent dialogue in the game after Map 8, and the least rewarding.

**Too little:**
- **Silent maps:**
  - Map 18 says nothing at all.
  - Maps 22-24 say nothing.
  - Maps 6, 9, 12, 15, 16 and 17 have one conversation of 2-3 lines.
  - These maps make up the long stretch of Forge grinding, the same 16-day stretch where the player is waiting on Dust.
- **Boss banter:**
  - One generic two-line exchange ("boss_banter") in your side's voices, the same on every map.
  - It fires only on fights whose kind contains "boss": on the main road, 5-7, 10-9, 11-R, 15-7, 20-9, 20-10 and 20-R.
  - Most map enders are elites with the boss role (for example 3-7 and 1-5), so they get none.
- **No map has an arrival line.** The map's blurb is the only greeting.

**Proposals (decision ECON2-9):**
- One curiosity of 2-4 lines on each silent main map: 18 and 22-24 first, then 12, 15, 16 and 17. Claude drafts from `docs/lore-bible.md`, the owner edits.
- Errand offers at half the rate, with twice the pay (ECON2-6).
- Let boss banter fire on map enders too: `roadEnderKind` / `node.ender`. Better still, give each map's ender a one-line taunt in place of the shared exchange. This is a later writing task, not in the decisions.

**What reads badly:**
1. **Fights under the title panel: 15 main maps and 5 side maps.**
   - The trail's second stop very often sits at the top left (y 7-12%). At desktop width the title panel covers it.
   - On Gannet Cliffs, 3-1 is barely visible. On the Sundered Peak, 20-2 and 20-3 sit under the panel and on top of each other.
   - At phone width the panel takes more of the canvas, not less.
2. **One layout on most maps.**
   - Maps 3, 5, 6, 9, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18 and 19 run the trail across the top edge, down the right edge and back along the bottom. The middle 40-60% of the canvas stays empty, and so does the left side below the title.
   - Maps 21-24 use zig-zags and diamonds that fill the canvas and read much better. So do Maps 10, 11 and 17.
   - Pebble Beach (Map 2) leaves its whole lower-left 60% empty.
3. **Crowding on the dark map.**
   - On Caves & Alcoves (Map 8), the fights 8-5 and 8-H sit 2.8% apart, with the pawn on top. The rest of the map is in darkness.
   - Its two feature spots (shop 2 and the Quest Board) are at the far top left, unlit.
   - It is the map where errands and the Quest Board open, and they are the hardest objects in the game to find.
4. **Entrances on the trail.**
   - The Clovermead entrance sits on 1-3 (4.8%).
   - The Basalt Foundry entrance sits on 19-3 (4.7%), and the eyrie curio on 11-5 (4.3%).
   - On Gatorback Swamp the Sunken Grove entrance sits on the trail between 5-6 and 5-7. On Pebble Beach the bottle curio sits on the line between 2-4 and 2-5.
5. **Empty maps for curiosities.**
   - Maps 15 and 22-24 have no feature, curiosity or other object. Map 18 has only its side-map entrance.
   - These are the maps that also say nothing (section 3), so one curiosity fixes both.

**Proposals (decision ECON2-10):**
- Fold the title panel to a small chip after the first look at a map (the blurb on hover / tap). This one UI change clears all 20 cases.
- With the layout editor, move these:
  - 3-1, 20-2 / 20-3 and 8-5 / 8-H apart.
  - The three entrances that sit on a fight.
  - The Sunken Grove entrance off the 5-6 → 5-7 line, and the bottle curio off the 2-4 → 2-5 line.
- When the maps are next re-laid, give Maps 3-19 the zig-zag or diamond trail that Maps 21-24 use, with the centre used.
- Put the new curiosities (ECON2-9) in each map's biggest empty area, so the map and the dialogue gain together.
