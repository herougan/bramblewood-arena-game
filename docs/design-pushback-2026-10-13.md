# Design pushback (2026-10-13)

You asked: is it too much, and is the game boring? These are my honest answers. Balance is still a work in progress, so this is about structure, not numbers.

## By the numbers

| Thing | Count today |
|---|---|
| Card skills and passives | 140 (117 passives) |
| Currencies | 8 (Maple Leaves, Gold Leaves, Dust, Metal, Krooni, Laurels, Kibble, plus Path points) |
| Map features to unlock | 19 |
| Board conditions | Day/night, twilight, moon, tide, high tide, wind, gale, three darkness levels, frost, heat, rain, fog, terrain tiers, eruptions |
| Progression tracks | Card levels, Forge tiers, Wildpaths, faction pools, journeys, ranks, Energy upgrades, account level |

## Is it too much? Yes, in three places

1. **Board conditions stack without the player choosing them.** A Map 11 fight can have terrain, wind, night, tide and a field card at once. Each is readable alone; together the result of a round is hard to predict, and a player who can't predict can't plan. Rule of thumb: **at most two conditions per fight**, and each map has one signature (the Tundra is frost, the Peak is wind, the caves are darkness).
2. **Too many currencies.** Eight is more than a new player can keep in their head; most live card games lean on two or three core currencies. Krooni, Laurels and Kibble each buy one thing. Suggest merging into **three**: a soft currency, a premium one, and Dust for crafting. Keep the others as items, not wallet entries.
3. **Skills that change numbers by +1 under conditions.** Many of the 117 passives are "+1 X when Y". They're individually fine, but a hand of them reads as noise. A skill earns its place if it changes **what you do** (where to play, when to play, what to target), not just a number.

## Is it boring? The risk is real, and it's in the core loop

- **Low decisions per turn.** One play a turn into a lane, then combat resolves itself. Most of the game's depth sits in deck building and conditions, not in the turn. In Hearthstone, Snap or Backpack Hero the fun is a meaningful choice every turn.
- **Auto-resolution hides the cause.** When a fight is lost to a miss chance, a wind round or a frost pick, the player doesn't feel outplayed, just unlucky.
- **What already works:** the lane placement and facing, Wait as tempo, pitching for Lumber, leaders, and the map's sense of a journey. The tutorial now teaches the first two well.

## What I'd do, in order

1. **Give each turn a real choice.** Two plays a turn from Map 5, or one play plus one cheap action (move a unit one lane, or pitch). The Actions-per-turn rule already exists; try 2 on a few fights and see if they feel better.
2. **Show the next round.** A small forecast strip: "Next round: wind from the left, frost on 2 units, Night falls." Randomness that's announced a round early becomes planning.
3. **One signature condition per map, two at most per fight.** Trim the stacking in the skirmish editor.
4. **Cut the currencies to three** before the Steam launch, and fold the rest into items.
5. **Retire the bottom 30 skills.** Use the Card check to find the ones that never move a win rate, and merge or delete them.

None of this is urgent for balance; it decides whether a new player stays past Map 5. The fastest test is a playtester's first 30 minutes: note every moment they ask "why did that happen?"
