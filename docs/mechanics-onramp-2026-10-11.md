# Introducing stats and mechanics along the road (2026-10-11)

How to teach each stat and skill across Maps 0–20 so the game keeps feeling new without ever burying the player. These are recommendations to argue with, built on the 21-map road in `card-list-2026-10-11.md`.

## The rhythm: see it, get it, twist it

Every new idea gets three beats, spread over two or three maps:

1. **See it.** An enemy uses it first, in a fight where it's the main thing happening. The first-time coach tip fires then (it already does for every skill), and the skirmish preview card carries a small **New skill** ribbon, so the player knows what to look for.
2. **Get it.** Within the same map, a reward card hands it to the player with a small number (Poison 1, Armor 1). Being on the receiving end first makes owning it feel like a prize.
3. **Twist it.** Two or three maps later, a fight pairs it with something that changes how it plays: its counter, a bigger number, or a combination (Poison plus Regen, Flying plus Night).

**Bosses** are the exam for their map's idea. **Elites** mix that idea with an older one, so earlier lessons stay alive.

## Budgets that keep it readable

- **One headline idea per map, two at most.** Act One (Maps 0–10) teaches about 25 skills; Act Two (11–20) adds about 20 more, and leans on combinations.
- **Small numbers before big numbers.** The first card with a skill carries it at 1 or 2. The boss of that map may carry 3 or 4. The big versions (Armor 5, Poison 4) wait for Act Two.
- **Counters arrive one map after the threat.** The player should feel a problem for a map before the game hands them the answer.
- **A whole-board rule per map** (a field, Night, Tide, a cursed cell) gives every map its own texture without adding card text to learn.
- **Fights stay 1–2 keywords per enemy card through Map 5.** Three-keyword cards are for elites and bosses until Act Two.

| 1 | Attack and Health | Tutorial stage 1, vanilla cards only, the castle as the target |
| 2 | Wait | Tutorial: a Wait-1 card next to a Wait-0 card, the ring ticking down |
| 3 | Lumber cost and pitching | Tutorial: a card you can't afford, then a pitch that pays for it (with the red "already pitched" reminder) |

| 6 | Card levels | After Map 0's boss: the first Forge visit, showing a +10% "major" level |

**Stat bands I'd aim for (commons, at level 1):**

| Maps | Typical Attack + Health at 0 Lumber / 0 Wait | Biggest cards |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | 5–7 | 2 Lumber, ~20 points |
| 3–6 | 6–8 | 3 Lumber, ~30 points |
| 7–10 | 7–9 | 3–4 Lumber, ~40 points |
| 11–15 | 8–10 | 4 Lumber, ~50 points |
| 16–20 | 9–11 | 4–5 Lumber, 60+ points |

That keeps a Map 0 reward useful through Map 5 or so, which matters because the deck is built from what you own.

## Keeping it interesting between new ideas

- **Remix nodes.** One fight per map in Act Two rebuilds an old map's lesson with new cards ("Thistlewood, remembered"). It's cheap to make and it rewards players who learned.
- **A riddle fight per map.** A fixed enemy board and a fixed hand for you: win in N rounds. It teaches one interaction precisely (Lantern at Night, Exile against Revive).
- **Fields as the surprise.** A map's main field can flip midway through the boss fight (Day to Night at round 6), so the same skills read differently.
- **Wildpaths as the long tail.** A passive (+1 Attack to small units, a second pitch once a fight) changes how old cards feel without new card text. Unlock one trail mark per map, and one capstone per act.
- **Name the lesson.** Each map's intro card (the veil) can carry one line: "In the Mire, the dead don't stay dead." Players remember a rule better when it has a sentence.

## Small changes this suggests in the game

1. A **New skill** ribbon on the skirmish preview card when the enemy deck has a skill you haven't met.
2. The coach tip for a skill fires the first time an enemy card with it is **summoned**, not only when it acts.
3. The Codex groups skills by the map that introduces them ("Skills you met on the Pond").
4. A per-skill **counter line** in its details ("Answered by: Reach, Anti-Air").
