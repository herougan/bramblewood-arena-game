# Performance: why the game got slow with many cards (2026-10-06)

**The report:** "after we stuffed the site with OpenGL and other effects, the site is now running slowly when many cards are in view. Maybe cards themselves shouldn't have OpenGL effects?"

## What I found

**Cards have no WebGL of their own.** There is one WebGL canvas per surface (the battlefield floor, the splash, the map), never one per card. Everything on the cards is CSS. So more cards don't add WebGL work; they add CSS paint work.

I measured a battle with 12 cards on the board, moving the pointer across them for about 2.5 seconds. The figure below is main-thread work per test window, in Chrome's own metrics.

| Setup | Main-thread work | Main cause |
|---|---|---|
| Before the fixes | ~1,120 ms | Everything below |
| Atmosphere off | ~400 ms | The full-screen mote canvas and the blended tint were the biggest cost |
| Atmosphere and shaders off | ~380 ms | The shader adds GPU time more than CPU time |
| Snap guard off as well | ~385 ms | The snap guard is cheap |
| **After the fixes, everything on** | **~645 ms** | About 42% less than before |

**The three real costs:**
1. **The floating motes.** A full-screen canvas sat on top of everything and was redrawn every frame, building a new radial gradient for each particle.
2. **The live-clock tint.** It is a full-screen layer with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`. Whenever anything under it moves (every card animation), the browser re-blends the whole screen.
3. **Per-card CSS filters.** The name scroll on every card had a `drop-shadow` filter, so each card painted to its own offscreen layer.

**A trap I hit while fixing this:** a `body:has(...)` selector made style recalculation five times slower. It's now a plain class toggled from JavaScript, and I'll avoid `:has()` on `body` from here on.

## What changed

- **Motes:**
  - One pre-rendered sprite per colour, drawn with `drawImage`, at 30 fps.
  - **Paused completely during fights**, since the battlefield has its own weather.
- **Tint:** during a fight it switches to a normal-blend, 12% overlay. There's no multiply blending over animating cards.
- **Battlefield shader:**
  - Ambient-only frames draw at about 30 fps. Frames with an impact light or a shockwave still draw at 60.
  - Card-shadow positions are sampled every third frame.
- **Name scrolls:** no filter on board and hand cards; a cheap `box-shadow` instead.
- **Rain fallback:** an overlay, not a `filter`, on the battlefield.

## If it's still slow on your machine

Try these in order, and tell me which one helps:
1. Settings → Atmosphere → Off.
2. Settings → Shader effects → Off.

The first rules out the full-screen layers; the second rules out WebGL. If neither helps, the next suspect is the holo foil's pointer tracking on many cards at once. Holo only runs in the Nest and the card detail view, so that would point at a different screen.

## Follow-up 2026-10-07: card galleries (Codex, deck editor, Nest, Forge)

**The report:** "The deck editor and any page that shows the card gallery is laggy, esp the codex."

**Cause:** every card has a coloured band at its bottom that slowly drifts. The drift animates `background-position`, which means the browser repaints that card on every frame. With about 280 cards on a gallery page, that was about 280 repaints per frame, even with nothing happening.

**Fix:** the drift is paused by default. It only runs where few cards are on screen (the battlefield, your hand, the big single-card views) and on whichever card is under the pointer.

| Page | Work per test window before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Codex, idle (90 frames) | ~2,580 ms | ~170 ms |
| Codex, scrolling | ~4,380 ms | ~2,040 ms |
| Deck editor, scrolling (120 frames) | ~3,810 ms | ~420 ms |

The rest of the Codex's scrolling cost is drawing card art as it scrolls in. It's much cheaper on a real graphics card than in Claude's software-rendered test browser.

**Leaf sweep:** 9 leaves instead of 22, spread over about 1.1 s. There's no per-leaf filter, and the veil slides with a transform instead of repainting a gradient. Every part of the sweep now runs on the compositor, so it stays smooth while the next page is built underneath.
